Connection requirements

Email setup depends on your provider. Gmail, iCloud, Yahoo and Fastmail support app passwords when your account allows them. Google and Microsoft OAuth sign-in require your own client registration; Outlook.com requires this additional setup. Other connected services use credentials you supply.

Messages import is available in the current preview. It is Mac-only and reads text already synced to that Mac. It requires macOS Full Disk Access for Zelos; it does not import attachments or phone-only messages. WhatsApp uses an exported chat file. The current build can send a reviewed email reply when you explicitly choose Send reply and configure sending. Assigned AI tasks prepare unsent drafts.

04 / Your pace. Your rules.

You stay
in command.

Decide when Zelos reads, when it asks AI, and where your data goes. Useful controls, right where you need them.

01

Read now. Review when ready.

Choose when to check for changes, or configure automatic checks in Schedule. Checks run while Zelos is open. In the current preview, Read sources now imports fresh material without an AI call, ready for a separate review when you choose.

02

A backup you can hold.

Create and restore backups from Settings → Your data in the desktop app. Keep your archive, drafts, notes, history and settings together. Restore previews the contents and keeps a recovery copy.

Backups are not password protected and may contain credentials. Store them privately.

03

Know what is working.

See when a connection last read successfully. A warning takes you to the account that needs attention. Check for updates when you choose and review the release before installing.

06 / Built around your trust

Your life.
Your machine.

Your archive lives on your computer. There is no Zelos cloud account, no analytics and no crash reporting.

Read the privacy policy →

You choose the connections

Zelos contacts the mail, calendar and other services you add. Provider sign-in uses that provider’s services. Manual update checks contact GitHub for public release information.

You choose what AI can read

Online AI reviews and questions send selected archive content to your configured provider. Sharing controls can limit message text; subjects, addresses and snippets may still be included. A local model keeps the AI processing on your computer.

You make the final move

Zelos suggests priorities and prepares drafts. In the current build, sending a reply is a separate action you choose after reviewing it. Assigned AI tasks cannot send email or place a grocery order.

      Why does this happen?
      
      The Mac build's ad-hoc signature checks the app's internal consistency but does not
        verify who made it. Windows has no publisher signature on these installers. Neither a
        warning nor its absence guarantees that an app is safe. Published SHA-256 checksums let
        you compare your download with this release's files; they are not an independent security review.
      
      
      Two more things worth knowing. The Apple silicon build is single-architecture: an Intel
        Mac refuses it outright with a different message that has no Open Anyway in it,
        and Rosetta does not translate in that direction — take the Intel build from the plate
        above. And on macOS and Linux, Zelos locks its own files down to your account; Windows has
        no equivalent to set, so there the protection is the one your user profile already carries
        — which means an administrator on that machine can read it.

    For experts: running from source
    
      The zip is the whole program, and it is small enough to read. No bundled
      runtime, no packages, nothing to install. Unzip it, then run node zelos.mjs —
      Node 22.16 or newer, or 24 or newer. Not simply "22 or newer": Zelos
      searches with the SQLite that comes built into Node, and that build has no full-text search
      before 22.16 or anywhere in the Node 23 line. On one of those, Zelos says so and stops rather
      than half-starting. Then it opens in your browser at 127.0.0.1 and stays
      there.
      
    
      The two apps above are a different trade: around 100 MB, and Electron. The
      same Zelos core, wrapped in a Chromium window so that it has a tray icon and does not live in
      a browser tab — 97 to 126 MB to download depending on which one you take, and roughly 300 MB
      once it is unpacked on disk. The Windows plates are installers: they ask where to put it,
      then add a Start-menu entry and a desktop shortcut. The Mac plates are disk images you drag
      across. If a supply chain is the thing you are weighing, take the zip — it is the one with
      nothing in it but Zelos.
